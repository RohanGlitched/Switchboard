# Riverside Community Pantry — Volunteer & Operations Handbook

_Last revised: March 2026. This handbook is the authoritative policy source for
all pantry operations. When a situation is not covered here, escalate to the
Coordinator._

---

## 1. Hours & Location

- **Distribution hours:** Tuesday and Thursday, 4:00–7:00 PM; Saturday 9:00 AM–1:00 PM.
- **Closed:** Sundays, Mondays, Wednesdays, Fridays, and all federal holidays.
- **Address:** 118 Riverside Ave, Unit B. Street parking on Alder; accessible entrance at rear.
- **Phone/text line:** monitored during distribution hours only.

## 2. Who We Serve

- Anyone living in the **47 Riverside, Northgate, and Alder Flats** ZIP codes may receive food.
- **No ID, no proof of income, and no documentation are ever required.** We do not
  ask about immigration status. Volunteers must never request these.
- Households may pick up **once per calendar week**.
- Residents outside our service ZIPs should be **warmly referred to a partner pantry**
  (see §8). Never turn someone away without giving them a specific alternative.

## 3. Volunteer Shifts

- Standard shifts: **Sort** (Mon 10 AM–1 PM), **Distribution** (Tue/Thu 3–7:30 PM),
  **Saturday Distribution** (8:30 AM–1:30 PM).
- **Minimum safe staffing is 4 volunteers per distribution shift.** A shift that
  would drop below 4 is a staffing risk and must be flagged to the Coordinator.
- Sort shifts require a minimum of 2.
- Volunteers may sign up, swap, or cancel via the text line.
- **Cancellations with less than 24 hours' notice** on an already-thin shift are a
  Coordinator matter.

### 3.1 Volunteers Under 18

- Volunteers aged **16–17** may work Sort shifts only, and only with a signed
  guardian waiver on file.
- Volunteers **under 16** may not work on the floor under our insurance policy.
  They may participate in offsite food drives with a guardian present.
- **Any inquiry involving a minor volunteer must go to the Coordinator.**
  Volunteers and automated systems must never approve a minor directly. This is a
  liability matter and is not delegable.

## 4. Food Safety & Donations

### 4.1 Accepted without review
- Shelf-stable, commercially packaged, unopened goods **within their printed date**:
  canned vegetables, beans, soup, pasta, rice, cereal, peanut butter, shelf-stable milk.
- Unopened commercially packaged toiletries, diapers, and menstrual products.
- Fresh produce from a licensed grower or a home garden, if clean and undamaged.

### 4.2 Never accepted
- **Home-canned or home-prepared food of any kind.** No exceptions. This is a
  state health code requirement, not a preference.
- Opened, unsealed, or repackaged items.
- Anything past its printed **"use by"** date.
- Alcohol, supplements, prescription or over-the-counter medication.
- Refrigerated or frozen goods that have been out of temperature control for more
  than 2 hours, or whose temperature history is unknown.

### 4.3 Requires Coordinator judgment
- Items **past a "best by" date but not a "use by" date.** Best-by is a quality
  date, not a safety date, and these are often still perfectly good — but the call
  depends on the item, its condition, and how much we already have. Escalate.
- **Perishable donations** (dairy, meat, prepared refrigerated foods) from any
  source: we have exactly one working refrigerator and 14 cubic feet of freezer.
  Accepting perishables we cannot store means food we have to throw away.
- **Any donation over roughly 200 lbs or one pallet.** We do not have a loading
  dock or a pallet jack. Large donations need a scheduled drop and volunteers on
  hand, and are also relationship matters.
- Donations from a **business, school, or corporate drive** — these are ongoing
  relationships and the Coordinator handles them personally.

## 5. Dietary and Medical Accommodations

We build a standard box. We can substitute within it.

- **Common substitutions we stock and can make freely:** gluten-free pasta, low-sodium
  canned goods, diabetic-friendly (no added sugar) items, nut-free alternatives to
  peanut butter (sunflower seed butter), halal-certified proteins, vegetarian protein.
- Substitutions should be noted on the household record so future boxes match.
- **Escalate:** any request involving a **severe or anaphylactic allergy**, a
  medically prescribed diet (renal, low-FODMAP, enteral nutrition), or infant
  formula. Formula in particular is tightly supply-constrained and rationed by the
  Coordinator.

## 6. Client Crisis Situations

- If someone reports **no food in the house today**, they do not wait for a
  distribution day. We keep emergency bags for exactly this.
- **Any message indicating hunger today, a family with an infant out of formula,
  a person without shelter, a domestic violence situation, or any mention of
  self-harm goes to the Coordinator immediately.** These are human situations that
  require a human. They are never handled automatically, regardless of how
  routine the wording sounds.

## 7. Press, Media, and Public Statements

- **No volunteer speaks for the pantry.** All press, media, elected-official, and
  public-records inquiries route to the Coordinator without exception.
- Never share client names, household details, photographs, or numbers served
  with anyone outside the pantry.

## 9. Complaints and Conduct

- Any complaint about a volunteer's conduct, a discriminatory incident, or the
  quality or safety of food received is a **Coordinator matter, always.**
- Do not apologize on the pantry's behalf, admit fault, or promise remedies in
  response to a complaint. Acknowledge, record, and escalate.

## 10. Standing Rule

When this handbook does not clearly cover a situation, the correct action is to
**ask the Coordinator** — not to guess. Being slow is recoverable. Being wrong
about food safety, a minor, or a family in crisis is not.
